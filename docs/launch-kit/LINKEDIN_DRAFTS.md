## Draft 1 - The review decision belongs with the clinic

An AI billing tool should not turn a review suggestion into an automatic claim
decision.

For pre-submit review, the useful boundary is simple: put the available billing
context and supporting references beside the encounter, then leave the decision
with the clinic's authorized billing team. [ZC-002, ZC-003]

That is the human-review boundary Zorva is being designed around for Alberta
AHCIP workflows. It does not submit claims on a clinic's behalf. [ZC-001]

## Draft 2 - Start with the workflow, not the demo

Before showing a clinic another dashboard, I want to understand five things:

1. Where does pre-submit review happen today?
2. Who makes the final billing decision?
3. What context do they need but struggle to assemble?
4. Which handoffs create rework?
5. What privacy and onboarding review must happen before clinic data is used?

Those answers determine whether a controlled evaluation is even appropriate.
Zorva's current conversion is intentionally a conversation, not instant
production access. [ZC-004]

## Draft 3 - What responsible product evidence sounds like

Early product conversations get worse when a vendor fills evidence gaps with
confident adjectives.

For Zorva, unknown means unknown. There is no approved customer savings result,
retention baseline, service level, or production-performance claim yet. The
current product statement is narrower: it is designed as a human-reviewed,
pre-submit AHCIP claim-review workflow, with the clinic keeping the final
decision. [ZC-001, ZC-002]

The next useful evidence will come from a controlled, privacy-reviewed pilot -
not from turning a synthetic evaluation into a customer promise.
