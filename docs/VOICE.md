## Voice summary

Zorva sounds like a careful senior colleague: calm, specific, accountable, and
plain about limits. Confidence comes from showing what is known, what is
unknown, who decides, and what happens next. A number without approved evidence
is less credible than an honest boundary.

## Audience and job

| Reader | What they need from the writing | What to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Billing lead | A clear review step, available context, decision ownership, and next action | Teaching basic billing concepts, implying automatic submission, or asserting a problem before discovery |
| Practice manager or clinic owner | Fit, operating effect, accountable owner, and decision path | Unapproved savings, ROI, pricing, urgency, or workload claims |
| Privacy, security, or IT reviewer | Exact architecture and operating evidence, scope, date, and accountable source | “Compliant,” “secure,” residency, certification, or processor claims without approved evidence |
| Zorva operator | The record, owner, due action, safe boundary, and failure/recovery state | PHI in commercial notes, invented certainty, or hidden external action |

The initial market is Alberta primary care using AHCIP. Do not widen the reader
to North America, all Canadian clinics, every payer, or every practice without a
strategy decision and evidence.

## Message hierarchy

1. **Category:** human-reviewed pre-submit AHCIP claim review.
2. **Audience:** Alberta primary-care billing teams.
3. **Workflow promise:** bring available context and supporting references into
   review.
4. **Control boundary:** the clinic's authorized biller keeps the final
   submission decision.
5. **Current conversion:** start a conversation about fit, privacy, onboarding,
   and the present workflow.
6. **Proof:** show the implemented workflow and dated, scoped evidence; do not
   translate a synthetic evaluation into a customer outcome.

## Approved pattern lines

These are writing patterns, not permission to publish an asset:

- “Review Alberta claims with your billing team in control.”
- “Bring the available context into review and keep the final submission
  decision with your clinic.”
- “Start with workflow questions, not patient or claim data.”
- “The current public step is a reviewed conversation, not self-serve
  production access.”
- “Unknown means unknown; this source does not report the measure.”
- “This is local candidate evidence, not production or customer evidence.”

## Do and do not

| Do | Do not |
| --- | --- |
| Qualify the market and workflow | “Works everywhere” or “for every practice” |
| Name the human decision owner | “AI does the billing for you” |
| State that evidence is synthetic, local, or production when that distinction matters | Turn a validation-set metric into a customer-performance claim |
| Say a metric is unknown or unavailable | Fill the gap with an estimate, benchmark, or vanity zero |
| Link an exact approved source and review date | “Industry-leading,” “battle-tested,” or “enterprise-grade” |
| Describe the next approved step | “Get started today,” countdowns, or implied availability |
| Separate a privacy question from a privacy conclusion | “HIA compliant,” “HIPAA certified,” or “100% secure” |
| Use controlled-pilot language only after the offer is approved | Quote a duration, volume, price, discount, refund, or SLA from an old draft |

## Terminology

### Use when supported by the surface

- **AHCIP** - spell out Alberta Health Care Insurance Plan on first use for a
  general audience.
- **SOMB** - spell out Schedule of Medical Benefits on first use.
- **pre-submit review** - the current product category.
- **review item** or **finding** - use “finding” only where the product record
  actually exists.
- **available context** and **supporting reference** - do not imply complete or
  authoritative retrieval.
- **authorized biller** or **billing team** - names the decision owner.
- **controlled pilot** - only after the exact offer is approved.

### Avoid unless an approved claim requires the exact term

Revenue recovery, savings, ROI, denial reduction, undercoding rate, every claim,
all errors, real-time, autonomous, self-improving, compliant, certified,
region-pinned, on-shore, secure by design, scalable, robust, cutting-edge,
best-in-class, guaranteed, free trial, and production-ready.

## Numbers and evidence

A public number requires all of the following:

1. exact metric definition and population;
2. dated source and sample size;
3. evidence type (synthetic evaluation, local test, production operation, or
   customer outcome);
4. accountable owner and approval state;
5. allowed surfaces and review/expiry date;
6. surrounding limitations that prevent a misleading inference.

Repository engine metrics may describe a named evaluation artifact. They do not
prove clinic value, production latency, savings, denial reduction, willingness
to pay, or product-market fit.

## Length and mechanics

- Headline: preferably 60 characters or fewer, one idea.
- Supporting paragraph: two short sentences where practical.
- CTA: an explicit verb and destination; no exclamation mark.
- Product review item: state the question, source, uncertainty, and next action
  without prescribing unsupported billing behavior.
- Email: one purpose, one ask, no fake urgency.
- Use Canadian English in brand and marketing copy. Preserve official names and
  source wording when quoting a document.
- Prefer sentence case. Reserve all caps for compact eyebrow labels where the
  implemented design maintains legibility.

## Approval boundary

Good voice is not claim approval and draft copy is not send authorization.
Before an external asset is published or sent, verify its exact claims, owner,
surface, audience, attribution, expiry/review date, and action approval. Pricing,
spending, customer material, regulated representations, and sensitive-data
access retain their separate gates.
